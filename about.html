I built the system I couldn't find.

A founder story about abandoned planners, dopamine, and the day I stopped trying to fix my brain.

The diagnosis

I was 31 when a doctor told me I had ADHD. I laughed. I'd been running my own business for years — invoicing clients, shipping work, paying bills. "Fine" by any outside measure. Fine, except for the drawer full of abandoned planners.

The diagnosis didn't change who I was. It changed the story I'd been telling myself. I wasn't lazy. I wasn't undisciplined. I was running a business on a brain that works differently, using systems built for brains that don't.

The graveyard of systems

I tried everything. Everything failed.

  • GTD — read the book, built the lists, quit in week three.
  • Bullet journaling — bought the nice notebook, used nine pages.
  • Five planners — none made it past week two.
  • A wall of apps — Notion, Todoist, Trello, then a new one every month.

Every single one assumed the same thing: a brain that does what it's told. Mine doesn't. It needs small steps. It needs visual progress. It needs a reason to start that isn't shame.

That's not a moral failure. It's just how the hardware works.

I didn't need more discipline. I needed a system built for my brain.

The build

So I built my own.

One Notion page. Then ten modules. Task breakdowns that turn "write the proposal" into six checkable steps. A dopamine menu so finishing something actually felt like something. A body doubling log. Energy matching. Reset protocols for the bad days — because the bad days were coming whether I planned for them or not.

I stopped designing for the person I wished I was. I started designing for the person I am.

The first week, I shipped more of my own work than in the previous three months. The second week, I did it again. Not because I'd gotten more disciplined. Because the system finally matched the brain.

The test

Then I gave it away.

First to two friends. Then to a few more. Freelancers. Consultants. Coaches. Creators. People whose client work always got done — and whose own work never did.

Today, 200+ solopreneurs use Dopakit. The stories sound the same: "I stopped rebuilding my system every Monday." "I invoiced on time for the first time in a year." "I don't hate my planner anymore."

You don't need to change who you are. You need tools that fit who you already are.

What this isn't

One honest note.

I'm not a doctor. Dopakit isn't medical advice, and it won't replace treatment if you need it. It's a system built from lived experience and a decade of trial and error — and it works for a lot of people whose brains work like mine.

If you've been told your whole life that you just need to try harder, this is for you. Try a system that fits instead.
